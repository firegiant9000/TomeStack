## D5: the review UI (SPEC I-02)

UI: `src/Ui/src/components/ImportPanel.tsx` and `CandidateReviewPanel.tsx`, on the **Sources** screen, for each of the user's own sources with a PDF. Acceptance: the e2e test "reads the fixture PDF, reviews its candidates, and publishes an accepted one through the studio", run against the real DevHost and worker.

- **What the book is written for (items 3, 4):** a source that declares both rules families shows a group "This book is written for" with "2014 rules (SRD 5.1)", "2024 rules (SRD 5.2.1)" and "Both", none chosen. Both Read buttons are `aria-disabled` (never natively disabled, so keyboard users reach them) with the linked reason "Say what the book is written for." until one is picked, and a press is refused. They are also `aria-disabled` while a job runs ("Reading… — cancel it first or wait."), and "Read these pages" without a valid first page ("Enter a first page."); the pressed button keeps focus when the job starts. Resume is `aria-disabled` while another job runs; pressing Resume or Cancel moves focus to that job's progress line (`tabIndex=-1`) because the pressed button goes away. The answer goes to `import.start` as `writtenFor` (the family ids; "Both" sends both). A one-family source shows "Written for SRD 5.1 (from the source)." instead and sends nothing. The service refuses a `writtenFor` that is empty, unknown or outside the source (`import.family-not-in-source`, naming family ids only, and no job is written). The job stores it; a job without it (0.5.0) detects with the source's families.
- **Read the text and find candidates:** "Read these pages" (from and to) or "Read the whole document" starts a job. Its line shows progress while it runs, with Cancel and Resume. The line is a polite live region whatever the job's state, so the final status ("completed, …") is announced too. When it completes, it shows "Review N candidates".
- **Search the text of <source>** (SPEC I-03): pages with a snippet and "Open page N".
- **Candidates from <source>:** 50 at a time ("Previous 50", "Next 50"); filters for pages (from and to), kind, confidence (unsure first: below 80 %) and status (to review by default). Each candidate shows its name, kind, page, confidence and status.
- **A candidate** (a region named "Candidate: <name>", focused when chosen) shows:
  - its kind, page, family, confidence (labelled "a hint, not a check") and status;
  - **Open page N** (the PDF viewer; the desktop app only);
  - the **excerpt** beside it;
  - **what was read**, with unsure fields marked "(unsure)";
  - its **uncertainties**;
  - its **unresolved references**, each with "Dismiss";
  - an edit form: name, kind, a **Rules families** checkbox group (one box per family the source declares, ticked as the candidate has them; none ticked is allowed to save), text, and the proposed effects as JSON (they stay reference-only);
  - the **check** (blockers, the validator's problems, what it depends on);
  - **Accept as a draft**, which is `aria-disabled` until the check passes (never natively disabled, so focus is not lost when a re-check blocks it; the press is refused and `aria-describedby` points at the visible "Blocks accepting" or "Problems" list);
  - **Accept as reference**, blocked the same way, and **Ignore**.
- **Accept several at once (item 9, D33):** each to-review row has a checkbox ("Select <name>", a 24 px target). **Select the N shown** ticks the pending rows on the current page only (N is the real count, for example "Select the 9 shown"); **Clear selection** unticks them. **Accept selected as drafts** and **Accept selected as reference** call `import.candidate.acceptMany` (at most 200 ids, `confirm: true`, and `expected`: each id's `etag` as shown, where one stale row refuses the whole batch, R134): each candidate goes through the same provenance, blocker and validation checks as accepting one, the accepted ones are written in one transaction, and the others come back with their codes and are never forced. The status line says "Accepted 7; 2 need review.", and a list "Not accepted" says why each was skipped (by code, never quoting the document); those rows stay selected. The bulk buttons are `aria-disabled` with a linked reason until something is selected. A visually hidden `aria-live="polite"` paragraph says "N selected" once per change and "Selection cleared" when the page or a filter changes (the selection never outlives its rows). While the open candidate has an unsaved edit, the checkboxes and the bulk buttons are held with "Save or undo your edit first." like paging and the filters. A second press while a bulk accept runs does nothing. Accepted entries are drafts; publishing them is the studio's "Publish all drafts in this source…" ([homebrew-studio.md](homebrew-studio.md)).
- **Focus (WCAG 2.4.3):** a candidate that leaves the filtered list once reviewed (the default filter shows those to review) takes its details with it, so focus goes back to the "Candidates from <source>" heading.
- **Publishing** is the homebrew studio's, with its own validation: an accepted candidate is a draft in the source, listed there like any other.
- **What happens next (item 2):** a line under the "Candidates from <source>" heading says "Accepted entries are drafts in the Homebrew studio under <source>. Publish them there to use them in the builder." (plain text, always visible, not a live region), and "Open <source> in the studio" opens the studio on that source. A completed job that found candidates has the same button beside "Review N candidates", and only while that job's review is closed (the panel has its own). The panel's button is `aria-disabled` with the linked reason "Save or undo your edit first." while the open candidate's form differs from what is stored, because leaving would drop the edit. The studio's heading takes focus on arrival (WCAG 2.4.3), because the button that was pressed is gone with the Sources screen.

### Classes from a book (item 11, D34, ADR-016)

- **What is read.** The class table is rebuilt from the page, and the class's traits become proposals: hit die, saving throws, armor and weapon proficiencies, a subclass choice with no options, and, for a caster, `spellcasting` (the ability from the class's text, up to the next class: its table, headed "Features" or "Class Features", or its "Core ... Traits" heading, which the 2024 SRD prints a page before the table; the page holding it is not read, so a caster's own text on that page is missed and its ability is said as not found). Spellcasting is proposed only from spell slot columns (or Pact Magic's slots and slot level). A class whose table has no slot columns but whose text mentions spellcasting (a caster that tracks a highest spell level or a pool instead) gets the uncertainty "The class mentions spellcasting, but its table has no spell slot columns: add spellcasting by hand if it casts spells." Traits are read as "Label: value", as "Label value" with no colon, and from a two-column trait box (also one on the page before the class heading: only the lines under that class's "Core ... Traits" heading, down to the next "Core ... Traits" heading or the foot of the page, are read; a page with two classes is split by height, so the two-column layouts that put two trait boxes side by side still need your per-effect ticks). "All armor" is light, medium and heavy; "None" stops the armor search. A weapon line with a condition (a required property, "that", "which", "with") is not proposed and is said as an uncertainty, as is any missing saves, armor or weapons part. **Skills** are shown as read with an uncertainty, not proposed as a choice (R69): a skill choice needs installed option references per family, so the reviewer adds it in the studio.
- **Links.** Each name in the Features column links to the feature candidate of this import with that name that belongs to this class (it names the class, or sits on the class's pages up to the next class table), or becomes a reference stub of its own citing the class page (two classes naming the same feature get two stubs, also when both tables are on one page: each class is then read from its own part of the page, and a feature printed on that shared page is linked to neither, because the page cannot place it; the pages after it belong to the last class). Renaming a linked feature keeps the link. A name that matches exactly one installed published feature in the class's families (from another source) gets no stub; accepting pins that published revision (R67). Two or more matches, or none, give a stub. If, by the time you accept, such a name has no link and no single installed match (you changed the class's families, or a same-name feature was published since), it is not granted and the check says "N table features are not linked and will not be granted; add them in the studio" (a count, never names). A name whose feature was reviewed in an earlier import of the same PDF is not linked; the class says how many as an uncertainty (R68), and the note does not count it again.
- **Review.** The detail lists every linked feature with its levels, saying "installed", "already accepted" or "ignored: not granted" in words. Each proposed effect has its own "Confirm …" checkbox, unticked; ticked effects stay automatic, the rest reference. "Accept as a draft" is held, with its reason linked, until the hit die and, when there is one, the spell slot table are ticked. Saving an edit that changes the effects clears the ticks. A class in a bulk accept is held back with "is a class: open it, tick the hit die and slot table, and accept it on its own".
- **Accept.** One transaction writes the pending linked features as drafts, then the class draft with one automatic content grant per feature (at the lowest level the table gives). A feature already accepted is pinned at its newest published revision, else its newest draft. An **ignored** linked feature is not granted and does not block the class; the check says "N linked features are ignored and will not be granted" (R66). If a linked feature is reviewed between the check and the accept, the accept is refused (`candidate.linked-changed`) and nothing is written.

### From a book to a character

1. **Sources:** add a homebrew source written for the book's rules (in the studio), then attach the PDF.
2. **Say what the book is written for** ("This book is written for": 2014 rules, 2024 rules or both; a one-family source needs no answer), then **Read the whole document**.
3. **Review each candidate:** open its page, and fix its name, kind and **Rules families**. An armour row or class table the book does not place waits with no family until you tick one.
4. **Accept as a draft** or **Accept as reference**.
5. **Open <source> in the studio.**
6. **Publish each draft.** The builder then offers it.

Nothing becomes a rule until it is published there (ADR-004). For homebrew-heavy characters, see the [session-prep guide](session-prep.md).

## D4: dependency and confidence validation (SPEC I-02, ADR-004)

Service: `src/AppService/ImportCandidates.cs`. Acceptance: `tests/AppService.Tests/CandidateReviewTests.cs`, `ImportQuarantineTests`.

| Command | Payload | Does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `import.candidate.check` | `{ candidateId }` | **Writes nothing.** Builds the draft the candidate would become (through `CandidateQuarantine.ToDraftRevision`, the only conversion) and runs `ContentValidator` on it: schema, references, formulas and cycles. It also lists **dependencies**: the source, installed content it grants or offers (by name and pin), content it names that is missing, and unresolved names. And it lists **blockers**: each low-confidence field and unresolved reference. The result is `canAccept` and `canAcceptAsReference` |
| `import.candidate.edit` | `{ candidateId, expected, name?, kind?, rulesFamilies?, summary?, effects?, dismissReferences? }` | Saves the reviewer's version next to the detected one, which stays unchanged. `rulesFamilies` must be known and within the source's (`import.family-not-in-source`); it may be empty, and check/accept then report `import.family-needed`. Saving clears the low-confidence flags: the reviewer has seen the fields. References go only when dismissed by name, or when content of that name is installed. It is still a proposal |
| `import.candidate.accept` | `{ candidateId, asReference?, confirm: true, expected }` (`expected` is the candidate's `etag`, required, R134) | Creates a **draft** revision in the source. Refused without `confirm` (`candidate.confirmation-required`), while any blocker remains (`candidate.needs-review`), or when validation reports an error (`candidate.validation-failed`, with the validator's errors). **As reference:** the text and page without the proposed effects; blockers do not apply, and validation still runs |
| `import.candidate.ignore` | `{ candidateId, expected }` (the candidate's `etag`, required, R134) | Sets it aside; nothing is created |

- **Accepting never activates anything.** The draft is inactive (`content.unpublished`). Every effect is forced to `reference` by the quarantine, and the calculator never applies a draft. Making an effect automatic, and publishing, happen in the homebrew studio. Publishing is the existing, re-validating `content.publish`.
- **Accepting is one transaction:** the draft and the candidate's "accepted" status are saved together, so a crash cannot leave a draft behind a candidate that could then be accepted again.
- **Reviewed candidates stay reviewed** (`candidate.already-reviewed`), and each review step is in the job's audit log (`candidate-edited`, `candidate-accepted`, `candidate-accepted-as-reference`, `candidate-ignored`), without text.
- **Accept and ignore re-check inside their transaction.** Both read the candidate again there and refuse (`import.candidate-changed`, `candidate.already-reviewed`) if it was edited or reviewed since they started; accepting a class also refuses when a feature it grants changed since the plan (`candidate.linked-changed` for a status change, `candidate.linked-edited` for an edit; neither is `import.candidate-changed`, because the class itself did not change). That linked-feature check compares against the accept's own read of the feature, not against a token the caller sent (R140, accepted): one person edits both the feature and the class in the same panel, `candidate.linked-edited` covers an edit made between that read and the transaction, and an edit made after the check but before the accept rides into the class draft, which stays a draft for review. The review panel then asks for the class's check again and says "A feature this class grants changed while it was being accepted. The check has been refreshed; look it over and accept again." Nothing is written when refused. **Each of them checks the caller's `etag` (R134):** accept and ignore carry the `expected` token of the candidate as the review panel last read it, and accept-many carries one per id; the check runs before the transaction and again inside it, as for an edit. A missing or stale token is refused with `import.candidate-changed`, and one stale row refuses the whole accept-many and writes nothing (an id that no longer exists is still reported as blocked). Accept, accept-many and ignore return the row as stored, with its fresh `etag`. A refused accept or ignore has its own message ("Review it again"), and the review panel reloads the candidate and says it changed, as for an edit. A refused accept-many reloads the list, clears the selection (the tokens on screen are stale, so every retry would be refused) and says "Some selected candidates changed; the selection was cleared." in the polite status paragraph, with focus staying on the button that was pressed (R139).
- **A candidate's identity** (for "reviewed in an earlier import" and `import.already-accepted`) is its kind, name and first page, plus the class a reference stub belongs to, so two classes on one page that list one feature keep a stub each.
- **Error messages never quote the PDF's text.** A blocker counts the unresolved references instead of naming them; the review shows the names from the candidate itself.
- **Fixed during D4, before any release (ADR-004):** effect types added after content v3 (spell, weapon, armor) are typed only inside a revision of their schema version (ADR-003). A candidate read from storage, or sent by the UI, therefore carried them as unknown effects. The quarantine's "force to reference" did not reach their raw JSON, and the draft, once read back, had an **automatic** weapon or armor effect. The quarantine now reads the draft back before forcing every effect to reference, and stored candidates are typed on read. `ImportQuarantineTests.A_stored_candidates_versioned_effects_stay_reference_only_in_the_draft_and_after_it_is_read_back` covers it, through publish.

## D3: candidate detection

Code: `src/ImportWorker/Detection/CandidateDetector.cs`; the service runs it when a job's extraction completes (`src/AppService/ImportCandidates.cs`). Acceptance: `tests/ImportWorker.Tests/DetectionTests.cs` (the original fixture book), `ImportJobTests.A_completed_import_lists_its_candidates…`, and the measurement below.

**Deterministic and rule-based**, with no AI (an optional local model is M7). It reads the extracted blocks and their positioned lines, not PDF bytes, so it runs in the app. Most patterns are anchored, and every one has a 1 s timeout per match call (one shared constant, `DetectionLimits.RegexTimeoutMs`). Some calls run on a whole block or page of text. The timeout was 100 ms, which a busy computer could exceed on any call (R151).

| Detector | Recognizes | Proposes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spell | A name, then "Level 2 Evocation (Sorcerer, Wizard)" / "Evocation Cantrip (…)" (SRD 5.2.1 layout) or "2nd-level evocation (ritual)" / "Evocation cantrip" (SRD 5.1), then the Casting Time, Range, Components and Duration lines | A `spell` effect: level, school, the four details, concentration, ritual, class lists (5.2.1), the attack kind, a saving throw and the first dice, plus the description |
| Spell list page | A heading "<Name> Spells" (letters, digits, apostrophes, spaces and hyphens; at most 49 characters before "Spells") followed by a line "Cantrips" or "1st Level" ... "9th Level", then the spell names, one per line, until the next heading or a line that is not a name | Nothing of its own. Each spell candidate whose name is on the page gets the list key (the heading, normalised: "Fixture Seer Spells" gives `fixture-seer`) added to `lists`, marked unsure, with the uncertainty "Spell list 'fixture-seer' was read from page N; check it." and the "names no class lists" note removed. A heading that cannot make a valid key proposes nothing. A list carries to the next page when that page has no "<Name> Spells" heading before its first level group or name line (the uncertainty cites the heading's page); a new heading always replaces it. Class lists printed in a spell's own header ("(Fixture Mage)") go through the same normalisation (`fixture-mage`); one that cannot make a valid key is dropped, with an uncertainty that names the page only. Reviewers can add or change keys in bulk: "Add to a spell list…" in review (see [spellcasting.md](spellcasting.md)) |
| Feat | A name, then "Origin Feat" / "General Feat (Prerequisite: …)" (5.2.1), or a heading followed by "Prerequisite: …" (5.1) | "+N bonus to initiative / AC" modifiers and "proficiency in the X skill" grants read from the text |
| Class feature | "Level 3: Name" (5.2.1) or a name with "Level 3 X Feature"; in SRD 5.1 a bold heading or a run-in heading whose first sentence gives the level, and bold headings inside a "Class Features" section | Text only (the level and class as fields). Grants, resources and uses are added by hand |
| Weapon row | A row whose damage cell is exactly "1d8 slashing". Tables print as columns, so rows are rebuilt from line positions and work across a page break without the header | A `weapon` effect: category from the "Martial Melee Weapons" line above, attack, damage, type, properties, range, versatile dice and the 2024 mastery |
| Armor row | On a page with an armor section: "11 + Dex modifier", "14 + Dex modifier (max 2)", "17" or "+2" | An `armor` effect: category (from the section, or the column), Armor Class and the medium Dexterity cap |
| Class table | A "The Bard" or "Bard Features" title and a "Level … Features" table | A class with its features per level as fields, the rebuilt table, proposals (hit die, saving throws, armor and weapons, a subclass choice, spellcasting) and links to the features it names (item 11, D34; see [Classes from a book](#classes-from-a-book-item-11-d34-adr-016)) |

- **Third-party layouts (2026-10-14, items 5 and 6):** (1) A name printed in capitals ("FIXTURE BLADE OF DUSK") is proposed in title case ("Fixture Blade of Dusk", small words in lower case); the excerpt keeps the page's capitals. This applies to spell names, feat names, class-table titles ("THE NAME" or "NAME FEATURES") and the names of features that name their class ("Level 3 X Feature"); other class-feature names are left as printed. The class table's "Level" and "Features" header cells may be Title case or all capitals, not lower case. (2) A feat can be a name, a "Prerequisites: …" line (at most 400 characters) and the description, all in one block. That layout matches neither SRD, so with two families the feat gets **no family**, marked unsure, like armour rows; with one family it takes that family. Only the plural label counts there: a name followed by a singular "Prerequisite:" in one block is an option-list entry (an option-list entry with a singular "Prerequisite:" line, which measured as 20+ spurious feats on the SRDs). A block that starts with "Prerequisites:" is never a feat unless it directly follows a heading; a heading followed by a "Prerequisite(s):" block (any length) is the SRD 5.1 feat shape. A one-block feat is not also proposed as a class feature. (3) A weapon or armor row is dropped when its name holds a price ("5 gp") or reads as a sentence (a comma, then two or more lower-case words); a name with one word after a comma ("Fixture Lantern, small") stays. (4) The extractor also treats a font as bold when its name carries Black, Heavy, Semibold or Demi as a style token (the part after the last "-", "," or "+", or a camel-case word such as "…SansBlack"), so headings in such a face are recognised as headings; "Academic" and "Blackadder" are not bold. (5) A job resumed after upgrading extracts only its remaining pages, so its pages can mix the old and new heading rules; read the document again to apply the new rules everywhere. Measured on both SRDs with no change to any number; the third-party counts are in [m4-acceptance.md](m4-acceptance.md).
- **Every candidate** carries an excerpt (at most 4,000 characters), its page or page range, the proposed kind, name and rules family, the proposed effects, a confidence (0.05 to 0.99), uncertainties, the fields it read, **low-confidence fields**, and **unresolved references**: names it refers to that are neither installed (published content of the source's families) nor another candidate of the job. For example, "Fixture Stormcall" casts "Fixture Thunder Word".
- **Rules family:** the layout suggests one (the 2014 or 2024 style) for spells, feats and features, within the families the book is written for (the job's `writtenFor`, else the source's). A layout outside them falls back to those families, with an uncertainty. **Armour rows and class tables have no layout:** with one family they take it; with two (2026-10-14, item 3) they get **no family**, `rulesFamilies` marked unsure, and the uncertainty "Say which rules family this is written for before accepting." Check and accept are blocked with `import.family-needed` ("Say which rules family this entry is written for.") until the reviewer ticks one; that includes "Accept as reference". Pending armour rows and class tables that a 0.5.0 job (no `writtenFor`) found on a two-family source stay in that job's list with their silent `srd-5.2.1`, but check and accept now ask for a family too (`import.family-needed`, worked out when checked) until the reviewer saves a family. An edit that sends a families set stores the optional flag `familiesReviewed` on the candidate (absent otherwise, omitted from the JSON while false); a 0.5.0 edit never set it, so such a row still asks. Reading the document again makes a new list that asks properly. An entry already accepted (or accepted as reference) from another reading of the same document and source is refused with `import.already-accepted` ("This entry was already accepted from another reading of this document."), also computed when checked, so reading again never makes a second draft. Editing may leave the families empty (other fields can be saved first) but never names one outside the source (`import.family-not-in-source`, nothing saved).
- **Running headers and footers** (the same text, digits ignored, on at least a third of the pages) are ignored. **Known limit:** a heading printed on that many pages (for example a chapter title repeated on each of its pages) is ignored as well. No test covers that case yet.
- **Nothing becomes content:** candidates are stored per job for review (`import_candidates`, local only). A re-run replaces only candidates still pending. A later import of the **same PDF into the same source** does not propose again what an earlier import's review accepted or ignored (same kind, name and page; `ImportJobTests.A_second_import_of_the_same_PDF_does_not_propose_what_was_already_reviewed`).
- **Bounded in the app's memory:** detection reads the stored blocks and lines, not the joined text, up to 64 million stored characters (about 4,000 typical pages; `TomeStackApp.MaxDetectionChars`). A job past that detects its first pages only, and its audit says where it stopped (`detection-limited`); importing the rest as a page range detects them.
- **Cancellable:** detection stops when its job is cancelled, its PDF is removed or the app closes, and the job is `cancelled` or `interrupted`. Resuming it detects again.
- **A pattern that times out** is retried once: detection runs again, each pattern match with its own 1 s budget. A second timeout fails the job with `detect.timeout`. The pages stay stored and searchable. A hostile page therefore costs at most two runs.

`import.candidates { jobId, firstPage?, lastPage?, page?, kind?, minConfidence?, maxConfidence?, status?, offset?, limit? }` lists them in page order (then name) and writes nothing. It returns `{ items, total }`: `total` counts everything the filter matches, and `offset` (0 or more, `import.offset-out-of-range`) and `limit` (default 50, 1 to 200, `import.limit-out-of-range`) choose the page. A candidate matches a page range when it reaches `firstPage` or later and starts on or before `lastPage`; `page` is the same as setting both to one page.

**Review at scale (tester feedback item 13).** The job records two optional flags, omitted when they do not apply: `truncatedCandidates` (more than 5,000 candidates were found; the cap applies before the candidates you already reviewed are taken out, so it is reported as it is) and `detectionLimitedFromPage` (the first page the 64-million-character limit left out, the same page as the `detection-limited` audit event). The review panel shows 50 at a time with "Previous 50" and "Next 50", filters by "From page" and "To page", and says in plain text above the list "Showing the first 5,000 candidates; read fewer pages at a time to see the rest." and "Detection stopped before page N; read pages N on as a range to detect them." Changing a filter goes back to the first page. While the open candidate has an unsaved edit, the paging buttons, the filters and the other candidates are held with "Save or undo your edit first."; so are the open candidate's own Accept as a draft, Accept as reference and Ignore (R134), because they act on the stored candidate, not on the form (they stay focusable, with the reason linked, and a press does nothing). Acceptance: `CandidateReviewTests` (paging, range, limits, the command), `ImportJobTests.A_job_that_stopped_detecting_early…`, `DetectionTests.Detection_says_when_the_candidate_cap_was_hit`, `CandidateReviewPanel.test.tsx`.

### Precision and recall on the SRDs (measured 2026-09-28)

`SrdDetectionMeasurementTests` extracts both SRD PDFs, the same files whose hashes `licensing/srd-pack-review.md` records (the test checks them), and compares the candidates with the bundled packs. A match is the same kind, the same name (case, spacing and apostrophes aside), and a page within one of the pack's. The PDFs stay outside the repository, and the test skips without `TOMESTACK_SRD_PDF_DIR`. When it runs, it fails below floors set a little under the numbers below: precision 99 % and recall 99 % for spells, 95 % and 99 % for weapons, 70 % and 99 % for classes, and class-feature recall 85 % (5.1) and 99 % (5.2.1). Every matched spell's level and weapon's damage must be right.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Spells | 319 / 319, **100 %**, **100 %**; level right 319/319 | 339 / 339, **100 %**, **100 %**; level right 339/339 | The packs bundle every SRD spell |
| Weapons | 36 / 35, 97.2 %, **100 %**; damage right 35/35 | 38 / 37, 97.4 %, **100 %**; damage right 37/37 | The one extra in each is the blowgun, which the packs leave out on purpose (its damage is not a dice roll) |
| Classes | 12 / 9, 75 %, **100 %** | 12 / 9, 75 %, **100 %** | The extras are the Fighter, Monk and Rogue, real SRD classes the packs do not bundle |
| Class features | 179 / 115, precision n/a, recall **87.0 %** | 240 / 145, precision n/a, recall **100 %** | The packs hold only some classes and levels, so precision cannot be computed against them. By hand, the 5.2.1 extras are the "Level N:" features of the unbundled classes and levels. About two-thirds of the 5.1 extras are real features (Barbarian 4–20, Fighter, Monk, Rogue). The rest are subclass names and sub-section headings (circle terrains, "… Spells" lists), about 20 of 179. The 5.1 misses are subclass features with no level in their first sentence |
| Feats | 1 / 1 bundled; 1 candidate | 1 / 1 bundled; 17 candidates | The 17 are exactly the SRD 5.2.1 feats (the packs bundle one) |
| Armor | 13 rows | 13 rows | Not bundled, so not measured; 13 is each SRD's armor table (12 armors and the shield) |

**What these numbers do not show:** they measure the SRD layouts the detectors were written against. A third-party book with its own layout will score lower, especially on class features and tables. That is why every candidate needs review (ADR-004), and why the M4 exit gate runs a third-party test PDF (D6).

### Per-effect source spans (Import Lab L4, D64)

Each candidate says where on the page each proposed effect was read, so review can show that part of the page instead of the whole excerpt.

- `DraftCandidate.EffectSpans` is keyed by proposed effect id, and `NameSpan` places the name. Both are optional: a candidate stored before spans existed, or an effect the detector could not place, has none, and review then shows the whole excerpt as before.
- A `CandidateSpan` is `Page`, `PageEnd`, `BlockStart`, `BlockEnd`, `LineStart`, `LineEnd`, `PatternId` and `Confidence`. The blocks index the page's stored blocks (the page text the job keeps), so the text is read from there, not from the PDF. The detector keeps a span on one page (`PageEnd` is null) and sets no lines, for a PDF or for Markdown: **Markdown spans carry block indices only**, so `LineStart` and `LineEnd` are always null. The owner chose not to add a source-line field to `TextBlock`; the "Lines a–b" in review is worked out from the blocks' own lines on the stored page, not from the `.md` file's line numbers.
- `PatternId` is a stable id from `DetectionPatterns` (for example `spell.components`, `weapon.row`, `class.hit-die`): an id, never prose. `Confidence` is the candidate's own confidence for that pass; no pass scans a second time and no detection result changes.
- Spells place the effect on the header and its detail lines; feats place each bonus or skill proficiency on the paragraph that states it; weapon and armor rows on the table row; a class's hit die, saving throws and weapon and armor training on the line they were read from, and its spellcasting and subclass effects on that line, or on the table's "Features" header when the sentence is not on the class's page. A class feature proposes text only, so it has a name span and no effect span. A class's saves, hit die or training read from a Core Traits block on the page before the table have no span (a span never points off the candidate's pages).
- Stored candidates keep `CurrentSchemaVersion = 1`. `effectSpans`, `nameSpan` (inside `candidate` and `edited`) and `attachmentSha256` (on the row: the job's attachment hash when it was detected, so a span still resolves to the stored page text after the attachment is replaced) are additive keys, left out of the JSON while null, so a row stored without them reads and writes byte for byte as before. Accepting a candidate never reads them: the draft, its provenance page and its effects are exactly what they were. Editing keeps them.
- A 0.7.0-or-earlier build keeps `attachmentSha256` as an unknown field of the row (the stored candidate has a catch-all), but drops `effectSpans` and `nameSpan` when it saves the candidate (its draft candidate had no catch-all for unknown keys); review then shows the whole excerpt for that row. From this build on, a draft candidate keeps unknown keys too (`Extensions`, left out of the JSON while empty), so later additive fields survive a build that does not know them.
- A spell's effect span runs from its header through its detail lines and description, because its dice, attack and save are read from the description.
- Review shows the proposed effects as buttons beside the stored page text (`import.page` with the candidate's `attachmentSha256` when it has one, so the highlight stays on the PDF it was read from after the source's PDF is replaced; text nodes only, never HTML); choosing one highlights its span in a `<mark>` and says "Lines a–b". Review shows the whole excerpt, as before, whenever the candidate has no spans, the effect has none, the effect was edited away, the page fails to load, or the span does not fit the page's blocks.

### Possible duplicates in review (Import Lab L6)

A pending candidate that looks like content already in the library shows "Possible duplicate of {name} ({source})" beside its row in the candidate list. It comes from the read-only `content.duplicates { jobId }` command: the job's pending candidates (their current, edited version) against the latest published revision of each library content, inside one kind and one rules family. A candidate with no rules family is not compared. The chip is advice: accepting, editing and ignoring work as before, nothing is merged, and a failed lookup shows no chip and no error. The list asks again after an edit, an accept or a spell-list change. The classes and the rules are in [import-lab.md](import-lab.md) (IL-05).

### Markdown import (Import Lab L7, D67)

A homebrew source can import a `.md` file with "Import a Markdown file…" (no PDF needed). It feeds the same detector and review as a PDF; the differences:

- **Pages only.** The file is read once (up to 4 MB), its SHA-256 is kept on the job, and the worker kind `extractMarkdown` reads a temp copy outside the attachments folder that is deleted when the run ends. The file is not copied into the library, so it is not in library backups, and the job is not resumable: Resume is not offered and `import.resume` answers `import.markdown-read-again` ("Import the Markdown file again."). The job record carries `format: "markdown"` (absent for PDF jobs).
- **Errors:** `markdown.missing`, `markdown.unreadable`, `markdown.encoding` (not UTF-8), `markdown.too-large` (over 4 MB, 200,000 lines, 10,000 characters in a line, 2,000 pages or 40 table columns). Nothing partial is kept.
- **Search and stored pages.** A source with no PDF searches and shows stored pages from its newest Markdown job that stored pages (a newer failed or cancelled import does not hide an earlier good one); a PDF attachment's text wins when there is one. The in-app page view shows plain text with the page warnings in words (`markdown.page-order`, `markdown.front-matter-ignored`, `page.text-truncated`, `page.no-text`).
- **Import-derived.** Starting a Markdown import marks the source import-derived, like a PDF import, so it is never shared.
- **Re-imports.** Every edit-and-reimport of a `.md` stores a new page set (a new sha), and old page sets are not reclaimed.
- **Sparse pages.** A `<!-- page: N -->` marker starts page N (numbers must go up; a lower one is ignored with a warning). A page with no text is not emitted; a file with only warnings is one empty page.

| Markdown | Becomes |
|---|---|
| `#` to `####` heading | a heading block (large, bold text) |
| Paragraph | one text block, one line per source line |
| `Name. text` run-in head | a bold run-in head then body, as a PDF feature head |
| `-`, `*`, `+` or `1.` item | a list-item block |
| GFM pipe table | one block per row; cells at X = 72 + column × 90, header bold; a ragged table is plain text rows |
| `<!-- page: N -->` | starts page N; with no markers a page ends after 60 source lines |
| Front matter (`---` … `---`) | skipped, with a warning |
| Code fence | one plain-text block |
| Emphasis, code marks, links, images | text kept, marks and images removed |
| HTML | tags removed; `script`, `style` and comment content dropped; nothing is fetched or run |

Positions follow PDF conventions: Y decreases down the page, synthetic at 756 − 12 × row, so the detector's reading order and spacing rules work unchanged.

## D2: import jobs (ARCHITECTURE "Import lifecycle")

Service: `src/AppService/ImportJobs.cs`, database migration v6 in `SqliteStore`. Acceptance: `tests/AppService.Tests/ImportJobTests.cs`.

| Command | Payload | Does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `import.start` | `{ sourceId, firstPage?, lastPage? }` or `{ sourceId, wholeDocument: true }` | Starts a background job that extracts those pages of the source's PDF. **It changes no content.** Only the user's own sources (made in TomeStack) with an available PDF can be imported. Refused with `source.not-editable`, `source.no-pdf`, `attachment.missing`, `import.pdf-changed` (a linked file changed), `source.page-range-invalid`, or `import.busy` (one job at a time) |
| `import.status` | `{ jobId }` | The job: status (`queued`, `running`, `completed`, `cancelled`, `failed`, `interrupted`), page count, `nextPage`, pages done, unreadable, read by OCR, without text, and a failure code |
| `import.list` | `{ sourceId? }` | Jobs, newest first |
| `import.cancel` | `{ jobId }` | Stops the running job (the worker is killed). Pages already extracted stay |
| `import.resume` | `{ jobId }` | Continues a cancelled, failed or interrupted job at `nextPage`, on the same PDF (`import.pdf-changed` otherwise) |
| `import.audit` | `{ jobId }` | The local audit log: `created`, `started`, `resumed`, `page-skipped` (page and code), `detection-limited`, `cancelled`, `interrupted`, `failed` (with the code) and `completed` (with counts). **Codes, page numbers and counts only, never text from the PDF** |
| `import.search` | `{ sourceId, query }` | SPEC I-03: pages whose extracted text contains the query (2 to 100 characters, case-insensitive), with a snippet, at most 50. The text is the source's PDF attachment's when it has one, otherwise the newest Markdown job that stored pages. Only within one source; search across sources is M7 (BACKLOG B10) |
| `import.page` | `{ sourceId, page, sha256? }` | One extracted page: text, blocks, warnings and error. With `sha256`, the stored page for that hash, only if one of the source's own import jobs read it (else `import.page-not-extracted`); without, the source's PDF attachment when it has one, otherwise the newest Markdown job that stored pages |

- **Progress and resume:** each page is stored as it arrives, in the same transaction as the job's counts. A cancelled or failed job resumes where it stopped. A job that was running when the app closed (or crashed) is `interrupted` at the next start, and resumes the same way. This is page-granular.
- **A page that kills the worker fails alone:** when the worker crashes, times out on a page, runs out of memory or sends more than a page can hold (`worker.crashed`, `worker.page-timeout`, `worker.memory`, `worker.message-too-large`), that page is stored as unreadable with the code as its error, and a fresh worker continues with the next page. After 5 such pages in one run (`TomeStackApp.MaxSkippedPagesPerRun`), the job fails with the last code, and `import.resume` continues after them. A failure before the document opens (for example `pdf.encrypted`) still fails the job. Acceptance: `ImportJobTests.A_page_that_kills_the_worker_fails_alone_and_the_rest_of_the_book_is_read` and `…After_too_many_pages_that_kill_the_worker…`.
- **Limits:** the extraction limits of ADR-009 (c), per run.
- **The PDF is pinned by hash:** pages are stored per PDF (SHA-256 and page), so a re-import of the same file replaces them. Each run hashes the file before it starts, so a linked PDF that changed on disk fails with `import.pdf-changed` instead of storing its pages under the old hash. A change *during* a run is not detected. Removing the PDF first stops a job that reads it.
- **Page navigation does not depend on it (ARCHITECTURE):** a failed or cancelled job leaves the attachment, "Open page", page import as reference and the removal preview as they were.
- **Never exported (ADR-009 (d)):** `import_jobs`, `import_pages`, `import_candidates` and `import_audit` are local. No backup or share contains them, and `ImportJobTests.Extracted_text_is_never_exported_in_a_backup_or_a_share` checks both. After a restore on another machine, attach the PDF and import again.

**The shipped worker in the smoke:** `scripts/smoke.ps1` has the built shell import its generated two-page PDF with `import.start`. The job must complete with both pages through `TomeStack.ImportWorker.Host.exe` next to `TomeStack.exe`, with no blocked request.

## D1: extraction (ADR-009 (a) to (c))

Code: `src/ImportWorker/Extraction/` (`PdfPigExtractor`, `WorkerProtocol`, `WorkerProcessExtractor`), `src/ImportWorker.Host/` (`TomeStack.ImportWorker.Host.exe`, `WindowsOcrEngine`), and the contracts in `src/ImportWorker/ImportContracts.cs`. Acceptance: `tests/ImportWorker.Tests` (`ExtractionTests`, `WorkerProcessTests`, `OcrTests`).

- **`IDocumentExtractor.ExtractAsync(path, scope)`** yields `DocumentOpened(pageCount)`, then one `ExtractedPage` per page in the scope:
  - `text`: the page's blocks in reading order, separated by blank lines, so a two-column page reads one column at a time;
  - `blocks`: text, a box in PDF points from the bottom-left corner, font size and a bold hint (page coordinates, ARCHITECTURE);
  - `width` and `height`;
  - `fromOcr`;
  - `warnings`: `page.no-text`, `ocr.unavailable`, `page.text-truncated`, `page.blocks-truncated`;
  - `error`: `page.unreadable`, or `page.ocr-failed` when the renderer or OCR throws on the page; a code only, never text.
- **Document-level failures** are an `ExtractionException` with a stable code, and the message never quotes the document:
  - `pdf.missing`, `pdf.not-a-pdf`, `pdf.too-large`, `pdf.encrypted`, `pdf.unreadable`, `pdf.too-many-pages`;
  - from the worker: `worker.missing`, `worker.limits` (its heap cap did not apply), `worker.page-timeout`, `worker.timeout`, `worker.memory`, `worker.crashed`, `worker.message-too-large`, `worker.protocol` (a malformed message, or pages out of order or outside the scope).
- **OCR:** a page with no letters is rendered by Windows.Data.Pdf and read by Windows.Media.Ocr (`fromOcr: true`). Each recognized line is a block, with font size 0 because OCR has none. Without an OCR language, the page is reported with `ocr.unavailable` and stays empty. TomeStack never invents text.
- **Isolation:** the app never parses a PDF itself. `WorkerProcessExtractor` starts `TomeStack.ImportWorker.Host.exe` and sends one JSON request line on stdin; the worker answers with JSON lines on stdout. There is no socket (ADR-006). The parent enforces:
  - at most 1 GiB and 5,000 pages;
  - per page, at most 5,000 blocks and 20,000 lines, and at most 200,000 characters in each copy of its text: the page text, the blocks' text and the lines' text;
  - a length limit on every line the app reads from the worker, derived from the page limits (about 10 million characters by default), checked before the line is held;
  - 60 s per page, 60 min per run;
  - a 1 GiB managed-heap cap in the child, which the child reports before it parses anything (the app refuses to go on otherwise), and a 1.5 GiB watchdog on the larger of its working set and private bytes;
  - pages in order and inside the requested scope, and `done` only after all of them.
  
  Cancelling, or stopping early, kills the child's process tree. The child also exits when the app does, so a crashed app leaves no worker behind. What the child writes to stderr is discarded, never logged.
- **A second request kind: form fields** (character-sheet import S1, `features/ddb-pdf-import.md`). A request with `kind: "formFields"` (no `kind`, or `extract`, is the page extraction above, unchanged) is answered with the `worker` line, one `fields` line (`{ name, type, page?, value?, checked?, selected?, onState? }` per terminal AcroForm field, the full name joined from its parents' partial names), then `done`. Code: `src/ImportWorker/Forms/` (`AcroFormReader` in the child, `WorkerFormReader` in the app), sharing the process start, heap-cap check, bounded line reader, watchdog and kill with `WorkerProcessExtractor` through `WorkerSession`. Its limits are a sheet's, not a book's: 20 MB, 50 pages, 30 s per line and per read; at most 2,000 fields, 20,000 characters in one name or value, and 1 MB of names and values in total (`ddb.too-many-fields`, `ddb.value-too-long`), checked in the child before anything is sent (PdfPig builds the form's fields with their values first, so that parse is bounded by the 20 MB file size and the worker's sheet-sized caps: a 256 MiB heap, 512 MiB in all, 30 s), and again in the app, which accepts exactly `worker`, one `fields`, `done` or `error` (`worker.protocol` otherwise) and bounds the `fields` line (six characters per counted character, 256 per field, plus 64 KiB). When the catalog has no `/AcroForm`, the fields are read from the pages' widget annotations instead (a real export had named widgets but no catalog form; names are joined from the widget's and its parents' `/T`, and type, flags and value are inherited). A PDF with neither, XFA only included, is `ddb.no-form-fields`. The file checks and `pdf.*` codes are the extractor's. It runs outside the import-job queue, and nothing reads it yet: S3 wires it to `ddb.read`. Acceptance: `FormReaderTests` and the form tests in `WorkerProcessTests`.
- **Shipping:** the shell references the worker, so `TomeStack.ImportWorker.Host.exe` and its assemblies sit next to `TomeStack.exe`. They are published self-contained with it. The shell now targets `net10.0-windows10.0.19041.0` and keeps its output folder `bin/<config>/net10.0-windows/`.

### Malformed input (SPEC Q-02, Q-04)

| Input | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Not a PDF, or empty | `pdf.not-a-pdf`, before parsing |
| Over the size limit | `pdf.too-large`, before parsing |
| Truncated at 10 %, 50 % and 90 % | `pdf.unreadable`, or the pages lenient parsing recovers; never invented text |
| Encrypted (standard handler, unknown password) | `pdf.encrypted` |
| 5,001 pages (or more than a configured limit) | `pdf.too-many-pages`, before any page is read |
| A page tree whose `/Count` claims 900 million pages | no hang, no crash |
| A content stream that is not valid Flate data | a page error, not a failed book |
| A decompression bomb (512 MB of whitespace in about 0.5 MB) | the run must fail with a worker limit (`worker.page-timeout`, `worker.memory` or `worker.crashed`), and the child ends. Measured: the page timeout stops it. In an import, that page alone fails |
| The worker over its memory limit, silent past the page timeout, cancelled, stopped early, missing, or sent a malformed request | `worker.memory`, `worker.page-timeout`, the child killed, `worker.missing`, `worker.bad-request` |
| The worker's heap cap | reported by the child and checked by the app (`WorkerProcessTests.The_worker_runs_under_the_heap_cap_the_app_sets`) |
| A worker line longer than the limit | refused before it is held (`worker.message-too-large`) |
| A page the OCR cannot read | `page.ocr-failed` for that page; the others extract |
